About this document. This use-case document has been prepared in support of IBM's formal production onboarding request to UIDAI. It describes IBM's role as an integration platform provider — not a direct consumer of UIDAI APIs — the target enterprise customer base, the credential model under which customers access UIDAI services through the IBM App Connect connector, and the specific business workflows the connector enables. All use cases are grounded in documented enterprise demand across BFSI, HR, government welfare, and gig-platform verticals. The connector is designed to be modular — supporting both customers who hold their own AUA/KUA licence (Track A) and customers who access Aadhaar services via a licensed intermediary such as IDfy or Signzy (Track B) — with the ability to switch tracks within the same integration flow as their regulatory posture matures.
⚠ Core Commercial Framework — IBM License ≠ Government Data Access

A customer purchases the IBM India Stack Connectivity Pack and receives pre-built, production-ready App Connect connectors for Aadhaar, DigiLocker, UPI, GSTN, Account Aggregator, eSign, ONDC, and ABHA. But the connector is just a pipe. The pipe carries no data unless the customer holds their own licence or registration with the underlying government authority. IBM's software licence covers the connector runtime (ACE BAR files, CP4I-certified). It does not confer the legal right to query UIDAI's CIDR, MeitY's DigiLocker, or NPCI's UPI rails on behalf of that customer's end users. IBM is the integration platform provider; the customer (or their licensed intermediary) is the legal credential holder; UIDAI is the data authority. These are three distinct roles, and IBM's onboarding request to UIDAI concerns only IBM's right to build, certify, and maintain the connector infrastructure — not to consume Aadhaar data on behalf of any end user.

❌ Customer
Has IBM subscription. Can run connectors. But has no UIDAI API credentials of their own unless they hold an AUA/KUA licence (Track A) or engage a licensed KYC-aaS intermediary (Track B). This gap is what IBM's modular connector design resolves.
✅ UIDAI / Govt API
Requires separate AUA/KUA licence held by a legally eligible entity (Section 4: banks, telcos, insurers, PMLA bodies) or access via a licensed KYC-aaS broker. UIDAI is the data authority and the approving body for production access.

Two credential tracks: Track A — AUA/KUA Direct Customer holds own AUA/KUA (banks, NBFCs, insurers, sovereign entities). IBM registers as Sub-AUA under the customer's UIDAI agreement (Circular 02/2025).    Track B — Brokered (IDfy / Signzy) Customer accesses Aadhaar services via a licensed KYC-aaS intermediary. IBM routes through their AUA-licensed API, adding consent audit trail, PII masking, and canonical schema. Connector is modular — customers can switch tracks in the flow.

2 / Aadhaar Offline KYC (OKYC / OVSE) — Integration value: UIDAI's Offline KYC (OKYC) via the Offline Verification Service Entity (OVSE) registration is open to any Indian company — IBM India Pvt. Ltd. qualifies directly with no AUA/KUA dependency and no HSM or CERT-In audit requirement. Under OKYC, the resident downloads their Aadhaar XML (digitally signed by UIDAI), shares it with the verifying party, and IBM verifies the UIDAI digital signature — no real-time biometric API call, no Aadhaar number transmitted. This covers approximately 65% of enterprise identity verification needs in HR onboarding, gig worker verification, vendor registration, and educational institution workflows. Enterprises hiring tens of thousands of employees annually currently collect physical Aadhaar copies, scan them manually, and email them through HR systems — creating fraud risk (tampered documents), 7–14 day onboarding lag, and no real-time UIDAI cross-check. IBM can hold the OVSE registration directly and begin delivering this use case within 4–6 weeks of registration.
