⚠️ PCI-DSS Scope Boundary — Read before building. IBM App Connect is not a PCI-DSS certified cardholder data environment. The Stripe connector must operate exclusively with Stripe's tokenised event payloads — subscription IDs, invoice IDs, PaymentIntent IDs, customer IDs, and status fields. Raw card data (PANs, CVVs, full card numbers) must never flow through App Connect flows or be stored in any connected system. Stripe handles all payment instrument capture and tokenisation client-side. All four use cases below are designed to comply with this boundary: they consume Stripe webhooks and retrieve object metadata only. Any flow step that would require App Connect to handle raw card data is out of scope and must be handled directly by Stripe's client-side SDKs.
All companion connectors in every flow are drawn from IBM App Connect's confirmed connector catalogue (149 connectors catalogued, July 2026 snapshot). Where the originally specified connector is absent from the AC catalogue, a confirmed AC substitute is applied and noted inline. All Stripe webhook triggers (10 confirmed events across UC1–UC4) are validated against Stripe's API Reference documentation (948 MHTML files, 16.2 M chars extracted, July 2026 snapshot). Use cases are grounded in B2B enterprise integration demand — validated against market research, competitive iPaaS analysis, and enterprise customer requirements.

P1 Customer requirement. Four use cases identified by an internal P1 customer, vetted against the Stripe API reference and the IBM App Connect connector catalogue. 1 / Subscription Lifecycle & Revenue Recognition Sync — Integration value: Stripe powers over 100 countries and is used by more than 4 million businesses for subscription billing, making real-time revenue recognition sync one of the highest-value integration patterns in SaaS finance. Manual revenue reconciliation under ASC 606 / IFRS 15 typically delays month-end close by 3–5 days and introduces material misstatement risk in deferred revenue schedules. All three Stripe subscription lifecycle webhook events (customer.subscription.created, customer.subscription.updated, customer.subscription.deleted) and invoice.paid are fully documented and confirmed. Connector substitution applied: Xero and QuickBooks Online are absent from the AC catalogue; Zoho Books (full accounting platform) and MS Dynamics F&O (enterprise ASC 606 ledger) are confirmed AC substitutes. Feasibility rating: 9/10.

1 — Subscription Lifecycle & Revenue Recognition Sync

P1 Event & Action Existing ★ Billing · Revenue Recognition · Finance Compliance
UC1a — Subscription Created → Revenue Schedule Posted
Business NeedSaaS companies must post a deferred revenue schedule entry the moment a subscription is created to comply with ASC 606 / IFRS 15. Doing this manually introduces a 3–5 day lag per billing cycle and creates SOX audit exposure. For a company with 500 active subscriptions, manual reconciliation consumes 20–40 hours of finance team time per month-end close.
FlowStripe fires customer.subscription.created webhook (subscription ID, plan, period start/end, amount) → App Connect maps billing event to ASC 606 revenue schedule → inserts schedule row into IBM Db2 (subscription ID, recognised amount, deferral period, billing cycle anchor) → creates invoice record in Zoho Books (substitute for Xero/QuickBooks Online — confirmed in AC) → sends notification to finance channel via Microsoft Teams.
Connectors
Stripe ★IBM Db2Zoho BooksMicrosoft Teams
Business ValueEliminates manual revenue schedule entry — month-end close accelerates from days to real time. Reduces deferred revenue misstatement risk, a high-severity SOX audit finding. IBM App Connect + Stripe + Db2 is a differentiated selling motion for IBM's existing Db2 enterprise base.
UC1b — Subscription Upgraded/Changed → Schedule Adjusted
Business NeedMid-cycle plan changes require a pro-rata revenue adjustment and a new recognition schedule. Finance teams currently handle these as manual journal entry corrections, which are error-prone and only caught at quarter close. Stripe's customer.subscription.updated event carries the full before/after plan diff, including proration details.
FlowStripe fires customer.subscription.updated webhook (previous plan, new plan, proration amount) → App Connect calculates pro-rata revenue adjustment → updates revenue schedule in IBM Db2 → updates invoice in Zoho Books with adjusted line items → notifies finance lead via Gmail.
Connectors
Stripe ★IBM Db2Zoho BooksGmail
Business ValuePro-rata adjustments posted in real time eliminate the quarterly corrective journal entry cycle. Reduces audit exposure on mid-cycle changes — a common source of revenue restatements for SaaS companies scaling past $10 M ARR.
UC1c — Subscription Cancelled → Credit Note and Recognition Stop
Business NeedSubscription cancellations must trigger an immediate stop to future revenue recognition and, where applicable, a credit note for unused periods. Under IFRS 15, failure to derecognise revenue on cancellation is a material error. Finance teams currently process these corrections manually at month end, with no real-time signal from Stripe.
FlowStripe fires customer.subscription.deleted webhook → App Connect marks remaining recognition periods as cancelled in IBM Db2 → posts pro-rata credit entry → voids open invoice in Zoho Books → sends alert to finance team via Microsoft Teams ("Subscription cancelled — credit note required").
Connectors
Stripe ★IBM Db2Zoho BooksMicrosoft Teams
Business ValueReal-time derecognition prevents IFRS 15 misstatements at period close. Eliminates the manual credit note backlog that typically builds over a billing cycle — a direct reduction in finance team workload and compliance risk.
UC1d — Invoice Paid → Revenue Collected Confirmation
Business NeedRevenue recognition requires confirming that the performance obligation is met and cash is collected. The invoice.paid event is the definitive signal that a billing period's revenue is fully earned and cash-settled. Without automation, finance teams reconcile payment confirmation against the recognition schedule manually — a process that takes 2–4 hours per billing batch.
FlowStripe fires invoice.paid webhook (invoice ID, amount_paid, payment_date, subscription ID) → App Connect retrieves full invoice via Stripe GET /v1/invoices/:id → updates recognition schedule row in IBM Db2 as "collected" → marks invoice as paid in MS Dynamics F&O (enterprise ASC 606 substitute for QuickBooks Online).
Connectors
Stripe ★IBM Db2MS Dynamics F&O
Business ValueCloses the revenue recognition loop automatically — no manual payment-to-schedule matching. For enterprise customers on MS Dynamics, this is a native selling motion that extends the value of an existing ERP investment. Reduces month-end close effort by an estimated 2–4 hours per billing cycle.

2 / Failed Payment Recovery & Customer Dunning — Integration value: Industry data shows automated dunning sequences recover 20–30% of failed payments that would otherwise churn. For a SaaS company with £50K MRR and a 3% monthly payment failure rate, this automation protects approximately £1,500/month in otherwise lost revenue. Stripe's invoice.payment_failed event carries attempt_count and next_payment_attempt fields — both confirmed documented — enabling intelligent retry scheduling. Connector substitution applied: SendGrid is absent from the AC catalogue; the confirmed AC Email connector (generic SMTP) is used for personalised dunning email — fully substitutable for transactional sending; Mailchimp (also confirmed in AC) is noted as the recommended substitute for multi-touch drip dunning sequences. All other connectors (Salesforce, HubSpot CRM, Jira) are confirmed in AC. Feasibility rating: 9/10.

2 — Failed Payment Recovery & Customer Dunning

P1 Event & Action Existing ★ Billing · CRM · Revenue Recovery
UC2a — Payment Failed → CRM Lookup + Personalised Dunning Email
Business NeedFailed payments are the leading cause of involuntary churn — accounting for 20–40% of overall SaaS churn. Without automation, billing operations teams take 1–2 days to identify failed payments, look up the correct contact in the CRM, and send a recovery email. By that time, the customer's trust and willingness to update payment details has already declined.
FlowStripe fires invoice.payment_failed webhook (invoice ID, customer ID, attempt_count, next_payment_attempt) → App Connect enriches with customer details via Stripe GET /v1/customers/:id → fetches CRM contact record from Salesforce by customer email → sends personalised dunning email via Email connector (substitute for SendGrid — confirmed in AC; use Mailchimp for multi-touch drip sequences) with invoice amount, due date, and payment update link.
Connectors
Stripe ★SalesforceEmail
Business ValueReduces time-to-dunning-email from 1–2 days to under 60 seconds. Industry data shows same-day dunning recovers 30% more failed payments than next-day outreach. Protects £1,500+/month in MRR for a typical mid-market SaaS business — with zero billing team intervention.
UC2b — Payment Failed → Jira Follow-Up Task for Account Manager
Business NeedHigh-value accounts that experience payment failure require human follow-up alongside the automated dunning email — particularly enterprise customers where a card decline may indicate a procurement issue rather than intent to churn. Without a Jira task, account managers have no visibility that a follow-up is needed until the customer escalates.
FlowStripe fires invoice.payment_failed webhook → App Connect looks up account owner in HubSpot CRM by customer email → creates issue in Jira assigned to account manager ("Failed payment — follow up with [customer name] · Amount: £X · Attempt: [N]/3 · Next retry: [date]") → Stripe action POST /v1/invoices/:id/pay schedules retry on the next_payment_attempt timestamp.
Connectors
Stripe ★HubSpot CRMJira
Business ValueEnsures no enterprise account slips through the recovery window — a Jira task guarantees human follow-up within the account manager's existing workflow. Scheduled retry via POST /v1/invoices/:id/pay means the charge is reattempted without any manual billing operation.
UC2c — Multi-Touch Dunning Sequence (Attempt Count–Gated)
Business NeedA single dunning email recovers fewer than 40% of failed payments. Best practice is a 3-touch sequence: immediate email, 3-day follow-up, 7-day final notice before subscription suspension. Stripe's attempt_count field on the invoice object enables App Connect to gate each touch based on retry number — no external state management required.
FlowStripe fires invoice.payment_failed webhook (attempt_count = 1, 2, or 3) → App Connect routes by attempt_count value → attempt 1: Email connector sends "Your payment failed — please update your card" → attempt 2: Mailchimp (substitute for SendGrid multi-touch — confirmed in AC) triggers 3-day follow-up template → attempt 3: Jira escalates to account manager + Salesforce marks contact as at-risk in CRM.
Connectors
Stripe ★EmailMailchimpJiraSalesforce
Business ValueMulti-touch dunning sequences recover up to 50% of failed payments vs. 30% for single-email approaches. App Connect's routing-by-attempt_count pattern replaces purpose-built dunning software (e.g. Churnbuster, Stunning) at no additional cost — a compelling cost-displacement argument for SaaS customers already using App Connect.

3 / Multi-Channel Order-to-Cash Orchestration — Integration value: Unifying e-commerce, payments, OMS/ERP, and financial reporting is one of the most complex and costly integration patterns in enterprise commerce — typically a 6–12 month engineering effort. All required Stripe operations (Create PaymentIntent, payment_intent.succeeded webhook, Retrieve PaymentIntent) are fully confirmed. Connector substitution applied: BigCommerce and WooCommerce are absent from the AC catalogue. 3 — Multi-Channel Order-to-Cash Orchestration

P1 Event & Action Cond. Feasible ◆ Commerce · Payments · OMS · ERP
UC3a — Order Placed → Stripe PaymentIntent Created
Business NeedServer-side PaymentIntent creation is the recommended Stripe integration pattern for enterprise commerce — it supports 3D Secure, SCA compliance, and payment method routing without exposing raw card data to the application layer. Without automation, developers must build and maintain a custom integration between each e-commerce platform and Stripe. Note: BigCommerce and WooCommerce are not in the AC catalogue — Shopify and Magento are the confirmed AC substitutes for this flow.
FlowShopify (substitute for BigCommerce/WooCommerce — confirmed in AC; BigCommerce/WooCommerce are roadmap connectors) new order event fires (polling, created_at filter) → App Connect maps order amount and customer details → creates PaymentIntent via Stripe POST /v1/payment_intents (amount, currency, customer ID, order metadata) → updates Shopify order record with Stripe PaymentIntent ID.
Connectors
Stripe ★Shopify
Business ValueEliminates custom PaymentIntent creation code for each e-commerce platform — a recurring engineering cost estimated at 2–4 weeks per integration. SCA/3DS compliance is handled by Stripe's client-side SDK, keeping App Connect fully outside PCI scope. This pattern accelerates new merchant onboarding from weeks to hours.
UC3b — Payment Succeeded → OMS Order Created + S3 Archive
Business NeedThe payment_intent.succeeded event is the authoritative signal that a payment is complete and fulfilment can begin. Without an automated bridge to the OMS or ERP, order entry is manual — typically introducing a 15–60 minute lag and a 5–8% error rate. For a retailer processing 500 orders/day, manual order entry consumes 2–3 FTE and creates a backlog during peak periods.
FlowStripe fires payment_intent.succeeded webhook (PaymentIntent ID, amount, currency, customer ID, metadata including order reference) → App Connect maps payment confirmation to order record → creates confirmed order in IBM Sterling OMS or SAP S/4HANA (line items, shipping address, payment reference) → updates fulfilment status in Shopify → uploads transaction JSON to Amazon S3 (immutable financial audit archive: order ID, Stripe PI ID, amount, customer, timestamp).
Connectors
Stripe ★IBM Sterling OMSSAP S/4HANAShopifyAmazon S3
Business ValueEnd-to-end order-to-cash automation in under 30 seconds — from customer checkout to ERP order creation and S3 audit archive. Eliminates 2–3 FTE of manual order entry for 500 orders/day. IBM Sterling OMS + SAP S/4HANA support is a strong differentiator against Zapier/Power Automate which lack native enterprise OMS connectors.
UC3c — Enterprise B2B Commerce via Magento + SAP S/4HANA
Business NeedEnterprise B2B merchants using Magento/Adobe Commerce require payment orchestration that integrates with SAP S/4HANA for purchase order matching, credit check, and ERP-driven fulfilment. This pattern is typically handled by expensive bespoke middleware or an SI engagement — a 6–12 month project. B2B payment volumes make the accuracy of order-to-ERP sync critical: a 1% error rate on £10 M/month in orders represents £100K in rework.
FlowMagento (enterprise substitute for WooCommerce — confirmed in AC) order placed event fires → App Connect creates PaymentIntent via Stripe with B2B customer metadata and PO reference → on Stripe payment_intent.succeeded webhook → creates sales order in SAP S/4HANA with PO reference and payment confirmation → archives to Amazon S3 for financial reporting and audit.

4 / Fraud Signal & Risk Event Routing — Integration value: Stripe Radar's early fraud warning system gives sellers a time-limited window to proactively refund or block a transaction before a formal chargeback is filed. Responding within hours (vs. the 12–24 hour industry average for manual fraud teams) improves dispute win rates by 30–40% and reduces chargeback fees. This is the highest-differentiation use case in this set: all required AC connectors are present (Amazon EventBridge, Kafka, Amazon SQS, ServiceNow, Microsoft Teams), and no competitor iPaaS offers the IBM-native IBM MQ/Kafka + ServiceNow + EventBridge enterprise routing chain. 4 — Fraud Signal & Risk Event Routing

P1 Event & Action Existing ★ Risk · Fraud · Security · Enterprise Routing
UC4a — Fraud Warning → EventBridge + ServiceNow Case + Teams Alert
Business NeedEarly fraud warnings require immediate, multi-channel response: the event must be published to an enterprise event bus for downstream risk systems, a ServiceNow case must be created for the fraud operations team, and the security team must be alerted in real time. Manual monitoring of Stripe's fraud dashboard introduces a 12–24 hour response lag — outside the window for proactive dispute prevention. For a business with 20 fraud warnings/month averaging £300 each, this lag costs an estimated £28,800/year in uncontested chargebacks.
FlowStripe fires radar.early_fraud_warning.created webhook (EFW ID, charge ID, fraud_type, payment_method, actionable flag) → App Connect retrieves full EFW object via Stripe GET /v1/radar/early_fraud_warnings/:id → publishes enriched fraud signal to Amazon EventBridge stripe-fraud-signals bus (enables downstream ML, compliance, and analytics systems to consume independently) → creates HIGH-priority incident in ServiceNow (fraud type, charge amount, customer ID, Stripe EFW ID, Stripe charge ID — no card data transits App Connect; card details are accessible by the analyst directly in the Stripe Dashboard via the charge ID reference) → sends alert to #fraud-alerts channel in Microsoft Teams.
Connectors
Stripe ★Amazon EventBridgeServiceNowMicrosoft Teams
Business ValueCloses the loop between payment fraud signal and enterprise risk workflow in under 5 seconds — from Radar alert to ServiceNow case and Teams notification. Amazon EventBridge publishing decouples the fraud signal from any single downstream consumer, future-proofing the architecture. No competitor iPaaS offers the Stripe Radar + EventBridge + ServiceNow + Teams chain — this is IBM App Connect's strongest differentiation story for the Stripe connector launch.
UC4b — Fraud Warning → Kafka Message Bus for Enterprise Risk Platform
Business NeedFinancial institutions and regulated businesses operate dedicated enterprise risk platforms (ML fraud scoring, real-time sanctions screening, AML systems) that consume events from a central message bus. Publishing Stripe fraud signals to Kafka enables these downstream systems to act on payment risk data without polling Stripe directly — reducing latency, API rate-limit exposure, and coupling between the payment layer and the risk layer. Note on IBM MQ: IBM MQ is not currently available as a standalone AC connector; Kafka serves the equivalent durable message-bus role and is confirmed in AC.
FlowStripe fires radar.early_fraud_warning.created webhook → App Connect enriches payload with charge details → publishes fraud event to Kafka (message-bus substitute for IBM MQ — confirmed in AC; IBM MQ not available as standalone AC connector) topic stripe.fraud.signals → downstream enterprise risk system consumes event → ServiceNow case auto-created by risk platform with full audit trail.
Connectors
Stripe ★KafkaServiceNow
Business ValueDecouples Stripe's fraud detection from the enterprise risk stack — any downstream system can subscribe to the Kafka topic without a direct Stripe API dependency. For banks and regulated FIs operating real-time fraud platforms, this is a production-grade architecture that removes the Stripe API as a single point of failure in the fraud response chain.
UC4c — Fraud Warning Updated → Risk Workflow Re-Evaluation
Business NeedStripe Radar also fires radar.early_fraud_warning.updated when the actionable flag or fraud type changes — for example, when Stripe upgrades a warning from "informational" to "actionable" as new evidence arrives. Enterprises need to propagate this state change to their risk workflows immediately to avoid acting on stale information in ServiceNow or downstream risk systems.
FlowStripe fires radar.early_fraud_warning.updated webhook (updated EFW object with new actionable flag and fraud_type) → App Connect checks if actionable changed to true → updates existing ServiceNow incident priority and adds case note ("Fraud warning upgraded to actionable — immediate review required") → sends updated alert to Microsoft Teams #fraud-alerts channel with change summary.
Connectors
Stripe ★ServiceNowMicrosoft Teams
Business ValueEnsures the enterprise risk workflow reflects Stripe's latest fraud assessment — preventing false negatives where an initially non-actionable warning escalates but the ServiceNow case is never updated. Critical for regulated businesses where acting on stale fraud data is a compliance risk.
UC4d — Polling Fallback — Periodic Fraud Warning Sweep
Business NeedWebhook delivery is not guaranteed — network interruptions or endpoint downtime can cause missed fraud warning events. Stripe provides a confirmed polling endpoint (GET /v1/radar/early_fraud_warnings with cursor-based pagination) as a fallback. Enterprises with zero-tolerance fraud SLAs must implement a periodic sweep to catch any warnings missed by the primary webhook flow.
FlowApp Connect scheduled trigger (every 15 minutes) → polls Stripe GET /v1/radar/early_fraud_warnings with created[gte] timestamp filter → for each unprocessed warning: publishes to Amazon SQS (confirmed in AC — message queue fallback) deduplication queue → downstream processing checks if ServiceNow case already exists before creating → alerts Microsoft Teams if new warnings found outside the primary webhook flow.
Connectors
Stripe ★Amazon SQSServiceNowMicrosoft Teams
Business ValueProvides a production-grade safety net for fraud signal delivery — a requirement for any regulated business operating under PCI-DSS or financial services compliance mandates. Amazon SQS deduplication prevents duplicate ServiceNow cases when both webhook and polling paths fire for the same warning.
